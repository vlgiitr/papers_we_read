The paper introduces GEPA, a method that improves an agnet by rewriting its prompts. It studies the model’s reasoning and tool use and sees the trajectory, then explains what went wrong. GEPA uses these explanations to create and test better prompts while keeping the most useful improvements. The core idea is to maintain a pareto frontier of prompts, which work well on different tasks and then mutating them by crossing prompts at the pareto front. This performs better than GRPO on average and is a lot more sample efficient. Overall, the paper shows that learning through written feedback can sometimes work better than learning from simple reward scores
